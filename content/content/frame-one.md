## The decision happens before the audio starts

Someone scrolling Instagram decides whether to stay before your voiceover finishes its first sentence. So the first frame isn't an intro, it's the whole pitch. If the viewer can't tell what the reel is about from a single glance at frame one, most of them are already gone.

On the SUBSTRATEIQ reels, average watch time sat around 8.5 seconds. Whatever matters most has to land inside that window.

## What frame one needs

1. **The topic, named.** Not "you won't believe this". Say the actual thing: the company, the product, the number.
2. **The stakes.** Why should a stranger care? Find the bigger fact (money, jobs, a record broken), never a bigger adjective.
3. **About ten words.** Two short sentences, or one with a number in it. No qualifiers. If it doesn't read in one glance, cut it.

> [!warn] Subtitles don't count
> Burned-in captions of the voiceover are not the hook. The hook is the headline sitting on screen at 0:00, and it has to work with the sound off.

## Re-hook every eight seconds

Retention sags in the middle. A scene change on its own won't fix that, because swapping the visuals while the story stays flat still loses people. A re-hook gives a fresh reason to stay: a new number, a surprise, a "but here's what nobody's saying". Roughly every eight seconds, something new has to arrive.

## Let length follow the story

A two-fact story makes a 15-second reel. Padding it to 40 seconds with filler just hands viewers more chances to leave. And if the source is a list (top 5 tools, 7 changes), make it a carousel instead, where each item gets its own slide.

```flashcards
Q: What must frame one show?
A: The topic named outright and the stakes, readable in a single glance.

A: Roughly every eight seconds.

```
